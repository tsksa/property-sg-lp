How to sell a condo in Singapore: steps, costs and what you keep

A condo sale is a private contract run by two law firms, not an HDB process. The steps are fewer than for a flat, but more of the terms are yours to negotiate.

By Joe Tay · District Director, ERA Realty Network · CEA R009618D

The short answer

Price the unit from recent caveats in your project, check whether Seller’s Stamp Duty applies, grant an Option to Purchase, and let your conveyancing lawyer take the sale to completion. Your cash proceeds are the price less the outstanding loan, the CPF refund with accrued interest, any Seller’s Stamp Duty, legal fees and agent commission.

How a condo sale differs from an HDB sale

What differsPrivate condoHDB flat
Who runs the processThe seller’s and buyer’s lawyersHDB, through its resale portal
Option termsAgreed between seller and buyerSet by HDB’s Option to Purchase
Minimum Occupation PeriodNone for private condosMust be met before selling
ValuationThe buyer’s bank values the unit for its loanHDB values the flat after the option is granted

Executive condominiums are different again for their first years: they are bought under HDB rules and carry their own occupation period. If you own an HDB flat rather than a condo, start with how to sell an HDB flat.

Step 1: check what you would owe before you price

  • Seller’s Stamp Duty. It applies if you sell within four years of a purchase made on or after 4 July 2025, or within three years of one made between 11 March 2017 and 3 July 2025. See Seller’s Stamp Duty: rates and rules.
  • Your outstanding loan. Ask your bank for the redemption amount, and whether your loan has a lock-in period, a redemption notice period or a clawback of subsidies.
  • Your CPF refund. Check the principal and accrued interest for each owner in your CPF home ownership dashboard.

Step 2: price it from caveats in your own project

URA publishes caveats lodged for private home sales. Recent sales of the same bedroom type in your project, adjusted for floor, facing, size and condition, are the evidence a buyer and their bank will look at. Start with condo prices by district, which are built from URA caveats, then ask for a free valuation if you want it done for your stack.

A buyer who needs a loan can borrow only against the lower of the price and the bank’s valuation. A price well above what the banks will support narrows your buyers to those with spare cash.

Step 3: decide how you will sell

You may sell on your own or appoint an agent. CEA publishes checklists and standard contract templates for owners who transact on their own, and says to consider an agent if you are unfamiliar with the process. Commission is not fixed in Singapore; it is agreed between you and the agent. Property agent commission explains what to compare.

Step 4: grant the Option to Purchase

When you accept an offer, you grant the buyer an Option to Purchase (OTP) in return for an option fee. The buyer then has the option period to exercise it by signing and paying the balance of the deposit.

No agency sets these terms for a private sale. By market convention the option fee is 1% of the price, the option period is 14 days, and the buyer pays a further 4% on exercise, making a 5% deposit. Each of these can be negotiated, and whatever you agree should be written into the option. If the buyer does not exercise the option in time, it lapses and you normally keep the option fee.

The date the buyer accepts the option is, in most cases, the date IRAS uses to measure your holding period for Seller’s Stamp Duty.

Step 5: your lawyer takes it to completion

Each side appoints a conveyancing law firm. Yours holds the deposit as the option provides, answers the buyer’s legal requisitions, arranges redemption of your loan and the CPF refund, pays any Seller’s Stamp Duty, and apportions property tax and management fees up to the completion date.

Completion is commonly set 8 to 12 weeks after the option is exercised. That is a convention too, and both sides can agree a shorter or longer period, which matters if you need to line it up with your next purchase.

What comes out of the sale price

ItemWhat to know
Outstanding housing loanRepaid to your bank at completion. Check lock-in and notice terms early.
CPF refundCPF Board requires the principal withdrawn and its accrued interest to be refunded to your own CPF account. It is your money, but not cash in hand.
Seller’s Stamp DutyOnly if you sell within the holding period.
Legal feesAgreed with your law firm.
Agent commissionAgreed with your agent; GST is added if the agency is GST-registered.
Property tax and management feesYours up to the completion date, then the buyer’s.

CPF Board says that if the sale proceeds are not enough to cover the refund, you do not need to top up the shortfall in cash provided the property is sold at market value. It also counts option money you received in cash as part of the selling price.

If you are buying your next home as well

The order matters more than the sale itself. Buying before you sell can mean paying Additional Buyer’s Stamp Duty on the next home; selling first can mean renting in between. Work through sell first or buy first and check the duty with the stamp duty calculator before you grant an option.

Official sources and review date

Reviewed 15 Nov 2026 against CEA’s Buying or selling a private residential property, IRAS’s Seller’s Stamp Duty for Residential Property and CPF Board’s Sales proceeds after selling your home. Option fee, option period and completion time are market conventions, not rules, and are described as such above.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to sell a condo in Singapore?

Once a buyer exercises the Option to Purchase, completion is commonly set 8 to 12 weeks later, and both sides can agree a different period. Finding the buyer comes before that and depends on price, condition and demand in your project.

Do I need to meet an MOP before selling a private condo?

No. Private condos have no Minimum Occupation Period. Check Seller’s Stamp Duty instead: it applies if you sell within four years of a purchase made on or after 4 July 2025, or within three years of one made between 11 March 2017 and 3 July 2025. Executive condominiums have their own occupation rules.

Do I have to refund CPF when I sell my condo?

Yes. CPF Board requires the principal you withdrew and its accrued interest to be refunded to your own CPF account. If the proceeds are not enough to cover it, you do not need to top up the shortfall in cash provided the property is sold at market value.

Is the option fee for a private property fixed?

No. For a private sale the option fee, the option period and the exercise deposit are agreed between seller and buyer. A 1% option fee, a 14-day option and a further 4% on exercise are market conventions, not rules.

Important

This guide is general education, not legal, tax or financial advice. IRAS, HDB, URA and CPF Board apply the prevailing rules to the facts of your own case; check the official page or ask a conveyancing lawyer before you commit.
