Seller’s Stamp Duty in Singapore: rates, holding period and who pays

Seller’s Stamp Duty is charged when you sell a home within a set holding period. The rate depends on the date you bought it, and the rules changed on 4 July 2025.

The short answer

For a home bought on or after 4 July 2025, Seller’s Stamp Duty (SSD) applies for four years: 16% if you sell within the first year, 12% in the second, 8% in the third and 4% in the fourth. For a home bought between 11 March 2017 and 3 July 2025 the holding period is three years, at 12%, 8% and 4%. After the holding period there is no SSD.

What Seller’s Stamp Duty is

SSD is a duty the seller pays when a residential property is sold or disposed of within the holding period. IRAS applies it to residential properties and residential land acquired on or after 20 February 2010. It is separate from the Buyer’s Stamp Duty and Additional Buyer’s Stamp Duty a purchaser pays; use the stamp duty calculator for those.

SSD rates by purchase date

The rate is fixed by the date you bought the property, not the date you sell it. These are the two bands that cover almost every home sold today.

Holding period when you sellBought 11 Mar 2017 to 3 Jul 2025Bought on or after 4 Jul 2025
Up to 1 year12%16%
More than 1 year, up to 2 years8%12%
More than 2 years, up to 3 years4%8%
More than 3 years, up to 4 yearsNo SSD4%
More than 4 yearsNo SSDNo SSD

The July 2025 change lengthened the holding period from three years to four and raised each rate by four percentage points. IRAS states it applies to purchases on or after 4 July 2025, with no transition period. Older bands, for homes bought before 11 March 2017, are listed on the IRAS Seller’s Stamp Duty page.

How the holding period is counted

The period runs from the date of purchase to the date of sale. IRAS treats the purchase date as, in most cases, the date the Option to Purchase was accepted or the Sale and Purchase Agreement was signed; for a new HDB flat it is the date of the Agreement for Lease. The sale date is set the same way: usually the date your buyer accepts the option or signs the agreement, not the completion date.

That detail matters near an anniversary. A sale agreed one week before the third or fourth anniversary of your purchase is taxed at the higher band, even if completion falls after it.

What SSD is charged on

SSD is charged on the selling price or the market value at the date of sale, whichever is higher, and rounded down to the nearest dollar.

Worked example

You bought a condo in August 2025 and agree to sell it for $1,500,000 in October 2027. The holding period is more than two years and not more than three, so the rate is 8%. SSD is 8% × $1,500,000 = $120,000. Had the sale been agreed after the fourth anniversary, in or after August 2029, there would be no SSD.

Who pays, and when

The seller pays. IRAS requires payment within 14 days of the sale contract being executed, says stamp duty payment cannot be deferred, and charges a late-payment penalty. In practice your conveyancing lawyer pays it from the sale proceeds or asks you for the funds, so budget for it before you grant an option.

Does SSD apply to HDB flats?

Yes. HDB flats are residential property, and IRAS says that meeting the Minimum Occupation Period does not remove SSD if the sale falls within the holding period. For most owners the five-year MOP is longer than the SSD holding period, so the two rarely overlap, but they can where a flat is sold early with HDB’s approval. See selling an HDB flat after MOP for how MOP itself is counted.

Sales that are exempt

IRAS lists a small number of exemptions, including:

  • property compulsorily acquired under the Land Acquisition Act;
  • disposals in bankruptcy or the involuntary winding up of a company;
  • HDB flats returned to or taken back by HDB, including under the Selective En bloc Redevelopment Scheme;
  • HDB flats that owners must dispose of under HDB rules after an inheritance or a marriage;
  • foreigners required to sell under the Residential Property Act.

An ordinary change of plan, a job move or a need for cash is not an exemption. If you think one applies to you, confirm it with IRAS or your lawyer before you price the sale.

Before you sell inside the holding period

  1. Find your purchase date on the option or Sale and Purchase Agreement, and count forward to each anniversary.
  2. Work out the duty at the price you expect, and add it to your agent commission, legal fees and CPF refund.
  3. Compare the result with waiting. A few months’ delay can drop the rate by four percentage points of the whole price.
  4. If you are selling to buy, read sell first or buy first; the buyer’s duties on the next home can be larger than the SSD on this one.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Seller’s Stamp Duty in Singapore?

For a home bought on or after 4 July 2025, SSD is 16% if sold within one year, 12% in the second year, 8% in the third and 4% in the fourth. For a home bought between 11 March 2017 and 3 July 2025 it is 12%, 8% and 4% over three years. It is charged on the higher of the selling price and market value.

How long must I hold a property to avoid SSD?

More than four years if you bought on or after 4 July 2025, and more than three years if you bought between 11 March 2017 and 3 July 2025. IRAS counts from the date of purchase to the date of sale, which is usually the date the option is accepted or the agreement is signed, not the completion date.

Who pays Seller’s Stamp Duty, and when is it due?

The seller pays. IRAS requires payment within 14 days of the sale contract being executed and says payment cannot be deferred. Your conveyancing lawyer normally arranges it from the sale proceeds.

Does Seller’s Stamp Duty apply to HDB flats?

Yes. IRAS says meeting the Minimum Occupation Period does not remove SSD if the sale falls within the holding period. Because the usual five-year MOP is longer than the SSD holding period, the two rarely overlap in practice.
