Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD) rates

BSD applies to every residential purchase, HDB or private, on the higher of the price and market value. Each slice of the price is charged at its own rate, and the total is rounded down to the nearest dollar.

Worked examples: a $600,000 HDB resale flat pays $1,800 + $3,600 + $7,200 = $12,600 BSD. A $1,500,000 condo pays $1,800 + $3,600 + $19,200 + $20,000 = $44,600 BSD.

Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) rates

ABSD is charged on top of BSD, on the higher of the price and market value, according to who is buying and how many residential properties they will own. Joint buyers pay the highest rate that applies to any of them.

When stamp duty is due

Within 14 days after signing the document if it is signed in Singapore, or within 30 days after receiving it if it is signed overseas. IRAS charges penalties on late payment.

Common questions

How much is ABSD for a Singapore Citizen buying a second property?

20% of the purchase price or market value, whichever is higher. A Singapore Citizen's first residential property is exempt from ABSD (0%); a third or subsequent property is charged at 30%.

How much is ABSD for a Permanent Resident?

5% on a Permanent Resident's first residential property, 30% on the second, and 35% on the third and subsequent properties.

How much is ABSD for a foreigner buying property in Singapore?

60% of the purchase price or market value, whichever is higher, regardless of whether it is the buyer's first property in Singapore. Nationals of the US, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland are treated as Singapore Citizens for stamp duty under Singapore's Free Trade Agreements.

Is Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD) different for HDB and private property?

No. BSD uses the same tiered rate table — 1% to 6% depending on price — for both HDB resale flats and private residential property. ABSD, where it applies, is calculated on top of BSD.

How much stamp duty do I pay on a second property in Singapore?

Buyer's Stamp Duty at the usual tiered rates, plus ABSD: 20% for a Singapore Citizen and 30% for a Permanent Resident. A Singapore Citizen buying a second property at $1,500,000 pays $44,600 BSD and $300,000 ABSD, $344,600 in total.

What is Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD)?

SSD is charged when you sell a residential property within the holding period. For property bought on or after 4 July 2025, it is 16% if sold within one year, 12% in the second year, 8% in the third and 4% in the fourth, on the higher of the price and market value. No SSD is payable after four years.

When must stamp duty be paid?

Within 14 days after signing the document if it is signed in Singapore, or within 30 days after receiving it if it is signed overseas. For most purchases that document is the exercised Option to Purchase or the Sale and Purchase Agreement. Late payment attracts penalties.
