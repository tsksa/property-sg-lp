Buying a resale HDB flat or condo, with an agent on your side

The agent who shows you a listed home normally works for the seller. If you want someone working for you, Joe can act as your buyer’s agent, from setting the budget to completion.

By Joe Tay · District Director, ERA Realty Network · CEA R009618D
The short answer

For an HDB resale flat, get your HFE letter first, price flats from registered sales, then take the Option to Purchase. HDB caps the Option Fee at $1,000 and the Option Fee plus the Option Exercise Fee at $5,000, and gives you 21 calendar days to exercise. Completion is about 8 weeks after HDB accepts the resale application. A buyer’s agent is optional, and you agree the fee in writing before the search starts.

Who works for you when you buy

The salesperson advertising a home represents the seller. CEA says an agent cannot collect commission from more than one party in the same transaction, and that doing so is an offence. So the seller’s agent cannot also act for you. Source: CEA, buying or selling with a property agent.

If you appoint Joe, the scope and the fee go into CEA’s prescribed estate agency agreement for the purchase of residential property, signed before viewings start. CEA says there are no fixed commission rates. The commission guide sets out what to agree.

What Joe does for a buyer

  • Budget first. Works out the loan, CPF and cash the purchase needs from your HFE letter or bank in-principle approval, including any extra cash needed if HDB values the flat below the price.
  • Shortlist. Searches the towns or projects you name and checks each home against registered sales of the same flat type, storey band and remaining lease.
  • Price. Recommends an offer from those sales, and for an HDB flat, how likely the price is to sit above HDB’s valuation.
  • Negotiate. Deals with the seller’s agent on price, the option terms, the completion date and any items left in the home.
  • Paperwork. Takes you through the Option to Purchase, HDB’s Request for Value, the resale application or your lawyer’s conveyancing, stamp duty and completion.

Buying an HDB resale flat, in order

  1. Get an HFE letter. HDB requires a valid HFE letter before you obtain an Option to Purchase and again when you submit the resale application. It is valid for 9 months, and processing can take up to a month after HDB has all your documents. Source: HDB, HFE letter.
  2. Set the budget. Add up the loan, CPF and grants your HFE letter shows, and keep cash for the option fees and any amount above valuation.
  3. Price flats from registered sales. Compare the same flat type nearby, giving most weight to recent sales on a similar storey and lease.
  4. Take the Option to Purchase. The seller grants HDB’s prescribed OTP for an Option Fee of $1 to $1,000. The option runs 21 calendar days, including weekends and public holidays, and expires at 4pm on the 21st day. If you do not exercise it, the seller keeps the Option Fee. Source: HDB, Option to Purchase.
  5. Request HDB’s value. If you are using CPF or a housing loan, you submit a Request for Value by the next working day after the Option Date and pay HDB’s $120 processing fee. Any amount by which the price exceeds the value is cash over valuation, paid in cash. Source: HDB, Request for Value terms.
  6. Exercise and apply. You exercise the option by paying the Option Exercise Fee, which with the Option Fee cannot exceed $5,000. You and the seller then each submit your portion of the resale application, within the days agreed in the OTP and within 7 calendar days of each other. Source: HDB, Option to Purchase.
  7. Pay stamp duty and complete. Buyer’s Stamp Duty, and any Additional Buyer’s Stamp Duty, is paid to IRAS; for a document signed in Singapore the deadline is 14 days after signing. Source: IRAS, when to pay stamp duty. HDB says completion is about 8 weeks after it accepts the resale application. Source: HDB, resale completion.

Buying a resale condo

A private purchase runs through lawyers rather than HDB. Price the unit from URA caveats for the same bedroom type in the project. Your bank lends against the lower of the price and its own valuation, so a price above that valuation is paid in cash. For a private sale the option fee, option period and exercise deposit are agreed between buyer and seller. A 1% option fee, a 14-day option and a further 4% on exercise are market conventions, not rules. The condo selling guide follows the same transaction from the seller’s side.

Common questions

Do I need my own agent to buy a resale flat?

No. You may buy without one. The agent advertising a flat represents the seller, so appoint your own agent only if you want someone negotiating for you, and agree the fee in writing first.

Who pays a buyer’s agent?

The buyer who appoints them. CEA says an agent cannot collect commission from more than one party in the same transaction, and there are no fixed commission rates.

When do I need an HFE letter?

Before you obtain an Option to Purchase from an HDB flat seller, and again when you submit the resale application. It is valid for 9 months from the date of issue.

How long does an HDB resale purchase take?

The option runs up to 21 calendar days. After you exercise it, both sides submit the resale application, and HDB says completion is about 8 weeks after it accepts the application.
